## 9.16 Taipei
- **My write**

  Arrived at about 18:30. Geared up my bike till 19:30. Then went to ningxia night market, bought 蚵仔煎, 炸无骨鸡腿, 地瓜丸. After those dinner, went to xiangshan and took a little hiking (the first pic) up to a view point of Taipei 101 tower. Later walked to the tower, but the view is not matching with that from the view point. Then took the mrt back to the hotel.

- **polished**

  I arrived in Taipei at around 18:30 and spent about an hour assembling and setting up my bike. At 19:30, I headed to Ningxia Night Market for dinner and had **蚵仔煎 (oyster omelette), fried boneless chicken thigh, and sweet potato balls**.

  After dinner, I went to Xiangshan and did a short hike up to a viewpoint overlooking Taipei 101. I later walked over to Taipei 101 itself, but I actually preferred the view from Xiangshan. After that, I took the MRT back to the hotel.

- **English notes**
  - A few recurring things will make your English sound considerably more natural.
  - **“Geared up my bike” → “assembled/set up my bike.”** *Gear up* usually means preparing yourself/equipment for an activity, not physically assembling a folding bike.
  - **“Took a little hiking” → “did a short hike.”** Similarly, *went hiking* works, but *took a hiking* doesn't.
  - **“The view is not matching with…” → “I preferred the view from…”** Your original is understandable, but English normally wouldn't use *match* for comparing two views.

- **English notes**
  - **“Pried there” → “prayed there.”** *Pried* is the past tense of *pry* (撬开/打探). *Prayed* is 祈祷.

  - **“Replied some message” → “replied to some messages.”**
  - **“The rode is not bad” → “the roads were fine.”** *Road* = 道路; *rode* is the past tense of *ride*.
  - Also, for a diary describing a completed day, keep the narrative consistently in the **past tense**: *arrived, went, had, reached, booked, left*. That's probably the single biggest improvement you can make.

- **English notes**
  - **“a road cruising a forest”** → **“a road through a forest.”** *Cruise* describes what *you* are doing: *I cruised along a quiet forest road.*
  - **“better sightseeing”** → **“more scenic.”** For roads/views, *scenic* is exactly the word you're looking for.
  - **“I tested there”** → **“I rested there.”** Just a typo, but an important difference.
  - **“The view worth the climbing”** → **“The views were worth the climb.”**
  - **“My next step is 台中车站”** → **“My next stop was Taichung Station.”** *Step* is usually a stage in a procedure; *stop* is a place visited during a journey.
  - **“deciding road fork”** → **“the junction where I had to decide…”** Much more natural.
  - **“a long slowly climbing trip”** → **“a long, gradual climb.”** That's the natural cycling expression.

    - **“riding road around the lake”** → **“cycling path around the lake.”**
    - **“rent bike”** → **“rental bikes.”**
